Not a render

Printed and wired in a weekend

The head runs on one Atech board: an encoder as the ear, a neck motor, a speaker and a microphone. Taps are felt by its orientation sensor. The base holds an Arduino UNO Q whose camera finds a face and sends only numbers.

  • A press of the ear opens a talk, in the same voice as the phone.
  • The check-in runs only when asked. Talking has priority.
  • Every hardware part has a software twin, so the rest never waited for the print.

Architecture

Where every call goes

Voice goes straight from the device to Gemini Live. Everything Kaiku keeps goes to one small service on Modal, which asks Gemma on a GPU only when it needs to reason.

The challenges

One product, seven challenges

Six partner technologies and the main track, all in the same day of one person.

Aalto · Beyond the Clinic

A whole day, one record

  • Robot, phone, Mac and glasses feed four signal groups, each compared with her own usual.
  • A fixed, readable rule gives one consensus. Missing data never reads as stable.
  • The practitioner page is live at a public link, on a simulated record.

Best use of Gemini API

One voice, four bodies

  • Gemini Live speaks on robot, phone, Mac and glasses, and 18 tools let it move a ritual, a walk and a card.
  • 2.3 s from her last word to Kaiku’s first sound.
  • A call ran 4 min 48 s across a token renewal on a locked phone. TTS, embeddings and Lyria each do one job.

Best use of Gemma

Eight jobs, every answer checked

  • Gemma 4 does eight jobs over the record, from the practitioner review to the mind behind the voice.
  • Code checks every citation and writes every number; a trace shows each step.
  • Runs on Google’s API and on our own vLLM on a Modal H100.

Best use of Modal

The whole cloud, in two apps

  • One warm container serves the API, the dashboard and SQLite on a Volume.
  • Three schedules, and a .map() over 72 synthetic people.
  • Gemma on an H100 that scales to zero: a review in 5 to 10 s warm.

Meta · AI glasses

A walk, head up and hands free

  • Kaiku’s voice in the ear through the Device Access Toolkit and Bluetooth.
  • On real glasses: speaker, microphone and camera passed, and a walk sent 30 pictures to the conversation.
  • Seeing is opt-in, and the walk survives the glasses coming off.

Atech · Real World

A robot head on one board

  • Ear dial, tap sensing, neck motor, speaker and microphone on one 14-port board.
  • It runs the check-in with no laptop, and counted 47 taps in 50 s.
  • A live Gemini conversation plays through its speaker over MQTT.

Arduino UNO Q

Eyes that keep no pictures

  • The camera is read on the board itself: face, distance, room light.
  • Only numbers leave it, and a face wakes the Atech head, board to board.
  • The camera opens only after a spoken yes.

Reaches the care team

Numbers and evidence

Kaiku’s cloud rejects raw audio and images. Practitioners never see conversations or the notebook.

Every signal has its own switch. A crisis is never left to a model: the message is pre-written, with the MIELI helpline and 112.

Does it work?

On people: unknown. On synthetic people:

0.08

false flags per person-week

About one unneeded “worth a check-in” per person every three months.

96 %

of moderate changes flagged

Within 21 days. A sudden drop takes about two days, a slow slide about a week.

21 %

of mild changes reach “needs a call”

Mild change is mostly missed, and skipped check-ins hide a decline.

Our own generator, assumptions in docs/validation.md. These numbers describe the rule, not people. Nothing here is clinically validated.
